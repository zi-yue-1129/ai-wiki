# Circumplex Model of Affect (Russell 1980)

Russell (1980) proposed that emotional states can be placed on a circle defined by two dimensions, valence and arousal. Words such as "happy", "sad", "calm" and "angry" fall at particular points on the circle, and their relations follow from the angles between them. The model is a dimensional alternative to lists of discrete emotions, and it is still widely used to measure and to label affect in psychology and in affective computing.

This page is part of [Emotion and Affective Computing](../index.md). The parent page gives the overview; this page covers the method and its evidence.

## The problem

Emotion words are numerous and overlap. Researchers wanted to know whether the variety of emotional language could be reduced to a small structure, and whether that structure reflects how people actually experience emotion. A discrete list makes the boundaries between states hard to draw, and it does not explain why some emotions feel similar.

## The approach

Russell studied how people judge the similarity of emotion words. Participants rated how closely pairs of words related to each other, and the pattern of similarity was analysed to find the underlying dimensions. The analysis pointed to two axes, a pleasant-unpleasant axis (valence) and an activated-deactivated axis (arousal), arranged in a circle.

The steps were:

1. Collect judgements of similarity between emotion words.
2. Analyse the pattern to find the dimensions that best describe the words.
3. Arrange the words on a circle defined by valence and arousal, so that similar words lie close together.
4. Read the relations between states from their positions, such as opposite words lying across the circle.

Step 4 gives the model its explanatory power. A state is defined by its position, not by a label.

## Results

The paper reports that the emotion words fall into a circular arrangement defined by valence and arousal, and that the arrangement accounts for much of the similarity structure in the judgements. The results are qualitative in the summary used for this page. Check the full paper before quoting figures.

## Limitations

- The model is built from judgements of words, which may not match how people feel in real situations.
- Two dimensions leave out some distinctions, such as the difference between anger and fear, which share high arousal and negative valence.
- Measures based on the model depend on the scales used, and results vary across cultures and languages.

## Open questions

- **Are two dimensions enough?** Adding a dominance or control dimension improves some distinctions, and it is debated whether it is a basic axis.
- **Do the dimensions map onto brain signals?** Links between valence, arousal and neural measures are still being tested.

### Seminal papers

- Russell, J. A. (1980). A circumplex model of affect. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077714 — Proposes that emotional states lie on a circle defined by valence and arousal.
