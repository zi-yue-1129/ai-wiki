# Heuristics and biases (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974)

Judgement under uncertainty relies on a few heuristics that produce systematic errors.

## Problem

How do people judge probabilities and frequencies when they cannot compute the exact answer?

## Core idea

Judgement under uncertainty relies on a few heuristics, namely representativeness, availability, and anchoring and adjustment. Each is often useful, and each leads to systematic errors.

## Method

Illustrative problems and experiments, in which participants' judgements are compared with the answers that probability theory gives.

## Results

Judgements deviate in predictable directions from the normative answers. Examples include ignoring base rates when a description seems typical of a category, and estimates that are pulled toward an arbitrary starting value.

## Limitations

The heuristics are described in words rather than as a formal model, so the paper does not say exactly when each one applies. Later work has argued that many heuristics are adaptive, not merely biased.
