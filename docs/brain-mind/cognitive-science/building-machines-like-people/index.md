# Building machines that learn and think like people (Lake et al., 2017)

Three ingredients, causal models, intuitive theories and compositionality, are proposed as the route to human-like learning.

## Problem

Why do deep networks need far more data than people do, and how do they differ from people in what they learn and how?

## Core idea

Three ingredients are proposed: causal models of the world, learning grounded in intuitive theories of physics and psychology, and compositionality with learning-to-learn for fast generalisation to new tasks.

## Method

A target article with open commentaries, which argues for the three ingredients. Its evidence is a review of existing models and experiments, with a model that learns handwritten characters from a single example as a worked case.

## Results

The article is a position paper and does not report a new benchmark result.

## Limitations

The commentaries disagree about how much built-in structure machines need. Progress in large models since 2017 has changed how much of the argument still applies.
