# DeepSeek-R1

DeepSeek-R1 is a family of reasoning models from DeepSeek, described in a 2025 report. Its central finding is that reinforcement learning with rule-based rewards can teach a base language model to produce long, self-checking reasoning, without first training it on human-written reasoning traces. The family overview is on the [reasoning models](index.md) page, and the method is compared there with chain of thought, self-consistency and test-time scaling.

## Overview

Earlier reasoning models were typically fine-tuned on large sets of step-by-step demonstrations written by people or generated by another model. DeepSeek-R1 tests whether that supervision is necessary. Its first model, DeepSeek-R1-Zero, is trained with reinforcement learning from the base model directly. The main model, DeepSeek-R1, adds a short supervised start and further stages to improve readability and general behaviour.

## Problem

Writing reasoning demonstrations for every domain is expensive, and imitating them caps the model at the quality of the demonstrations. The report asks whether reasoning can be learned from a reward on verifiable outcomes alone.

## Core idea

Reward the model only for things that can be checked by a rule: whether a final answer is correct, and whether the reasoning appears inside the required tags. Optimise the policy with reinforcement learning. The abstract states that this improves reasoning without human-labelled reasoning trajectories, and that patterns such as self-reflection, verification and changing strategy emerge during training.

## Algorithm

The paper trains with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), introduced in DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024). For each question, the policy samples a group of \(G\) outputs, each receiving a reward \(r_i\). Each output's advantage is its reward relative to the group, so no separate value network is needed:

\[
A_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r_1,\dots,r_G)}{\mathrm{std}(r_1,\dots,r_G)}
\]

The paper's pipeline for DeepSeek-R1 has four stages:

1. **Cold start:** fine-tune the base model (DeepSeek-V3-Base) on a small set of thousands of long chain-of-thought examples, chosen for readability.
2. **Reasoning RL:** run RL on reasoning tasks. A language-consistency reward is added to the accuracy reward to reduce mixing of languages in the reasoning.
3. **Rejection sampling and supervised fine-tuning:** collect about 600,000 reasoning samples and about 200,000 non-reasoning samples, then fine-tune the base model on roughly 800,000 samples.
4. **RL for all scenarios:** apply rule-based rewards to mathematics, code and logic, and reward models to general data, aiming at helpfulness and harmlessness.

DeepSeek-R1-Zero skips stages 1 and 3. Its rewards are an accuracy reward and a format reward, the latter requiring the reasoning to appear in think tags.

## Results

- **AIME 2024, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1-Zero):** the paper reports the score rising from 15.6% to 71.0% during RL training.
- **AIME 2024, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1):** 79.8%.
- **MATH-500, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1):** 97.3%.
- The abstract also states that reasoning patterns from the large model can be used to improve smaller models.

## Limitations

- **Readability:** R1-Zero's outputs can mix languages and have poor formatting. The cold-start stage was added to address this.
- **Language:** R1 is tuned for Chinese and English, and it may reason in English for questions in other languages.
- **Prompting:** few-shot prompts consistently degrade its performance, and the authors recommend describing the problem and output format directly.
- **Software engineering:** long evaluation times limit large-scale RL on these tasks, so R1 improves little over DeepSeek-V3 there.
- **General abilities:** R1 falls short of DeepSeek-V3 on function calling, multi-turn dialogue, complex role-play and JSON output.

## Connections

- [Chain of thought](chain-of-thought.md) describes the prompting approach that R1 replaces with trained behaviour.
- [Self-consistency](self-consistency.md) and [test-time scaling](test-time-scaling.md) spend extra compute at inference. R1 spends it during training and at generation time.

## Open questions

- How much of R1's behaviour depends on the strength of the base model, which is already a capable model?
- Are rule-based rewards enough outside mathematics, code and logic, or does the general-purpose stage always need learned reward models?

## References

### Seminal papers

- DeepSeek-AI (2025). *DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948 — The report describing R1-Zero, R1, the training pipeline and the distilled models.
- Shao, Z., et al. (2024). *DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300 — Introduces GRPO, a PPO variant that drops the value network, used to train R1.
- Wei, J., et al. (2022). *Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903 — The prompting-based reasoning that R1 learns to produce on its own.
- Lightman, H., et al. (2023). *Let's Verify Step by Step*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050 — Step-level verification, a reward style that R1's rule-based reasoning rewards do not rely on.

### Surveys and textbooks

- Huang, J. & Chang, K. C.-C. (2023). *Towards Reasoning in Large Language Models: A Survey*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10403 — Surveys the reasoning methods that R1 builds on and the benchmarks used to evaluate them.
