# DeepSeek-R1

DeepSeek-R1 is a family of reasoning models from DeepSeek, described in a report published in *Nature* in 2025. Its central finding is that reinforcement learning with rule-based rewards can teach a base language model to produce long, self-checking reasoning, without first training it on human-written reasoning traces. The family overview is on the [reasoning models](index.md) page, and the method is compared there with chain of thought, self-consistency and test-time scaling.

## Overview

Earlier reasoning models were typically fine-tuned on large sets of step-by-step demonstrations written by people or generated by another model. DeepSeek-R1 tests whether that supervision is necessary. Its first model, DeepSeek-R1-Zero, is trained with reinforcement learning from the base model directly. The main model, DeepSeek-R1, adds a short supervised start and further stages to improve readability and general behaviour.

## Key concepts

- **Verifiable reward**: a reward computed by a rule, such as checking a final answer against a known result or checking that the output follows a required format.
- **Policy**: the language model being trained, which generates the reasoning and answer.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: the reinforcement learning algorithm used here. It scores each sampled answer against the average of its own group, and needs no separate value model.
- **Cold start**: a short supervised stage on long, readable reasoning examples, used before reinforcement learning in the main model.
- **Distillation**: training a smaller model on the outputs of a larger reasoning model, which the report uses to transfer reasoning behaviour.

## Problem

Writing reasoning demonstrations for every domain is expensive, and imitating them caps the model at the quality of the demonstrations. The report asks whether reasoning can be learned from a reward on verifiable outcomes alone.

## Core idea

Reward the model only for things that can be checked by a rule: whether a final answer is correct, and whether the reasoning appears inside the required tags. Optimise the policy with reinforcement learning. The abstract states that this improves reasoning without human-labelled reasoning trajectories, and that patterns such as self-reflection, verification and changing strategy emerge during training.

## Algorithm

The paper trains with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), introduced in DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024). The report's Section 2.1 describes it this way. For each question, the policy samples a group of \(G\) outputs, each receiving a reward \(r_i\). Each output's advantage is its reward relative to the group mean and standard deviation. The baseline comes from the group, so no separate value network is needed:

\[
A_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r_1,\dots,r_G)}{\mathrm{std}(r_1,\dots,r_G)}
\]

The paper's pipeline for DeepSeek-R1 has four stages:

1. **Cold start:** fine-tune the base model (DeepSeek-V3-Base) on a small set of thousands of long chain-of-thought examples, chosen for readability.
2. **Reasoning RL:** run RL on reasoning tasks. A language-consistency reward is added to the accuracy reward to reduce mixing of languages in the reasoning.
3. **Rejection sampling and supervised fine-tuning:** collect about 600,000 reasoning samples and about 200,000 non-reasoning samples, then fine-tune the base model on roughly 800,000 samples.
4. **RL for all scenarios:** apply rule-based rewards to mathematics, code and logic, and reward models to general data, aiming at helpfulness and harmlessness.

DeepSeek-R1-Zero skips stages 1 and 3. Its rewards are an accuracy reward and a format reward, the latter requiring the reasoning to appear in think tags.

## Key methods

- **DeepSeek-R1-Zero**: pure reinforcement learning from the base model, with accuracy and format rewards. It shows the effect on its own, but its outputs mix languages and read poorly.
- **DeepSeek-R1**: the cold-start, reasoning RL, rejection-sampling and general RL pipeline above. It is the model the family is named for.
- **Distilled models**: smaller dense models trained on samples from R1, which the report presents as a way to carry reasoning patterns into smaller models.

## Results

- **AIME 2024, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1-Zero):** the report says the average score rises from 15.6% to 77.9% during RL training. Table 3 lists 77.9% for R1-Zero. The report also notes that majority voting over samples raises this further.
- **AIME 2024, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1):** 79.8%.
- **MATH-500, pass@1 (DeepSeek-R1):** 97.3%.
- The abstract states that reasoning patterns of larger models can be systematically harnessed to guide and enhance the reasoning capabilities of smaller models.

## Limitations

The report's Section 6 lists the following limits, and the earlier sections explain the fixes.

- **Readability:** R1-Zero shows poor readability and language mixing. The cold-start stage and a language-consistency reward were added to address this.
- **Language:** R1 is optimised for Chinese and English, and it may reason and respond in English for questions in other languages.
- **Prompting:** few-shot prompting consistently degrades its performance, and the authors recommend describing the problem and output format directly in a zero-shot setting.
- **Software engineering:** long evaluation times slowed RL on these tasks, and R1 has not shown a large improvement over DeepSeek-V3 on software-engineering benchmarks.
- **Structured output and tools:** the report says its structured-output abilities remain weaker than existing models, and that R1 cannot use tools such as search engines and calculators.
- **Conversation:** the training data is mostly single-turn, which the report says may limit multi-turn conversational ability.

## Variants and more variants

- **R1-Zero**: no supervised start, the cleanest test of RL alone, and less readable.
- **R1**: the full pipeline, the main public model.
- **Distilled dense models**: smaller models trained on R1 outputs, which the report uses to compare reasoning transfer with direct RL on small models.

## Connections

- [Chain of thought](chain-of-thought.md) describes the prompting approach that R1 replaces with trained behaviour.
- [Self-consistency](self-consistency.md) and [test-time scaling](test-time-scaling.md) spend extra compute at inference. R1 spends it during training and at generation time.
- [Large Language Models](../llm/index.md) places this training stage after supervised fine-tuning and preference alignment.
- [Reinforcement learning](../../paradigms/reinforcement-learning/index.md) gives the general framework. The policy-gradient method used here is [GRPO](../../paradigms/reinforcement-learning/policy-gradient-and-actor-critic/grpo/index.md).

## Applications

- Competition mathematics, where answers can be checked against a known result.
- Code and logic tasks with automatic checks, which the report uses for its rule-based rewards.
- Distilled models as smaller reasoning models for settings where the full model is too costly to run.

## Open questions

- How much of R1's behaviour depends on the strength of the base model, which is already a capable model?
- Are rule-based rewards enough outside mathematics, code and logic, or does the general-purpose stage always need learned reward models?
- How much do the distilled models gain from R1 traces compared with training the same small model with RL directly?
