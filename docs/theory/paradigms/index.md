# Learning Paradigms

A **learning paradigm** describes *what kind of signal* a system learns from and *how* data arrives. Most real systems combine several: an LLM is pre-trained with self-supervision, fine-tuned with supervision (imitation of demonstrations), aligned with reinforcement learning, and adapted in context with few-shot examples.

## Overview

| Paradigm | Learning signal | Data arrival | Typical example |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Supervised](supervised/index.md) | A correct label for every input | Fixed dataset | Image classification, credit scoring |
| [Unsupervised](unsupervised/index.md) | Structure in the inputs alone | Fixed dataset | Clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation |
| [Semi- & weakly supervised](semi-supervised/index.md) | A few labels, or noisy/coarse labels, plus lots of unlabelled data | Fixed dataset | Medical imaging with few expert labels |
| [Self-supervised](self-supervised/index.md) | Labels manufactured from the data itself | Fixed (huge) dataset | Next-token prediction, masked image modelling, contrastive learning |
| [Reinforcement](reinforcement-learning/index.md) | Scalar reward, often delayed | Generated by interacting | Games, robotics, RLHF |
| [Imitation](imitation/index.md) | Expert demonstrations of behaviour | Demonstration logs (+ optional interaction) | Robot manipulation, driving, SFT of LLMs |
| [Transfer / domain adaptation](transfer/index.md) | Knowledge from a source task or domain | Source then target | Fine-tuning a pre-trained model |
| [Meta-learning / few-shot](meta-learning/index.md) | Experience across many tasks | Task distribution | Learning a new class from five examples |
| [Continual](continual/index.md) | Sequence of tasks or a drifting stream | Sequential, non-stationary | Personal assistants, robots that keep learning |
| [Active](active/index.md) | Labels the learner chooses to request | Learner queries an oracle | Reducing annotation cost |
| [Online & bandits](online/index.md) | Feedback after each prediction or action | One sample at a time | Recommendation, ad selection, clinical trials |
| [Federated & private](federated/index.md) | Any of the above, but data stays distributed or protected | Across devices / institutions | Keyboard prediction, multi-hospital models |
| [Other paradigms](other/index.md) | Multi-task, curriculum, multiple-instance, PU learning, in-context learning, … | Varies | — |
| [Probabilistic & Bayesian](probabilistic/index.md) | Prior beliefs, updated by evidence through Bayes' rule | Any data, with explicit uncertainty | Gaussian processes, topic models, probabilistic programming |
| [Symbolic AI, search & planning](symbolic-ai/index.md) | Hand-written rules and knowledge, plus search over states | A problem specification and a knowledge base | Expert systems, theorem proving, classical planning |
| [Evolutionary & swarm](evolutionary/index.md) | Fitness scores for a population of candidate solutions | Successive generations of candidates | Genetic algorithms, neuroevolution, particle swarm optimisation |

## How they relate

```mermaid
flowchart TB
  subgraph Signal[What is the signal?]
    S[Labels] --> SUP[Supervised]
    S2[Few / noisy labels] --> SEMI[Semi- / weakly supervised]
    S3[Data itself] --> SSL[Self-supervised]
    S3 --> UNS[Unsupervised]
    S4[Reward] --> RL[Reinforcement]
    S5[Demonstrations] --> IL[Imitation]
  end
  subgraph Setting[How does knowledge flow?]
    T[Transfer] 
    M[Meta]
    C[Continual]
    A[Active]
    O[Online]
    F[Federated]
  end
  SSL -->|pre-training| T
  T --> SUP
  IL -->|initialise| RL
```

The *signal* paradigms (left) say what the learner is told; the *setting* paradigms (right) say how tasks and data are organised over time, space and agents. They are orthogonal: you can do continual self-supervised learning, federated reinforcement learning, active semi-supervised learning, and so on.

## The brain uses all of them

| Paradigm | Biological / psychological analogue |
|---|---|
| Supervised | Cerebellar error-driven learning (climbing-fibre error signals); a teacher correcting a child |
| Unsupervised / self-supervised | Cortical statistical learning, predictive coding; infants segmenting speech from transition probabilities |
| Reinforcement | Basal ganglia and dopamine reward-prediction errors; operant conditioning |
| Imitation | Observational learning (Bandura's Bobo doll studies); the mirror-neuron debate; cultural transmission |
| Transfer | Applying old skills to new problems; analogical reasoning |
| Meta-learning | "Learning to learn" (Harlow's learning sets, 1949); prefrontal cortex as a meta-RL system (Wang et al., 2018) |
| Continual | Lifelong learning without catastrophic forgetting, supported by hippocampal–cortical consolidation |
| Active | Curiosity, question-asking, eye movements chosen to gain information |
| Online | Every moment of life is one sample at a time |

Doya (1999) proposed a division of labour — **cerebellum: supervised; cortex: unsupervised; basal ganglia: reinforcement** — that remains a useful first approximation. See [NeuroAI](../../brain-mind/neuroai/index.md) and [Memory & Learning](../../brain-mind/memory-learning/index.md).

## Choosing a paradigm

1. **Do you have labels?** Plenty → supervised. Few → semi-supervised, active, or transfer from a pre-trained model. None → self-supervised or unsupervised.
2. **Is the target behaviour easier to demonstrate or to score?** Demonstrate → imitation. Score → reinforcement learning.
3. **Does the data change over time?** Yes → online or continual learning, with drift monitoring.
4. **Can the data leave its owner?** No → federated learning, differential privacy.
5. **Do you need to adapt to many new tasks quickly?** → foundation model + in-context learning, or meta-learning.

## References

### Seminal papers

- Doya, K. (1999). *What are the computations of the cerebellum, the basal ganglia and the cerebral cortex?* Neural Networks, 12(7–8). https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(99)00046-5 — Proposes the cerebellum, basal ganglia and cortex as supervised, reinforcement and unsupervised learners.
- Wang, J. X., et al. (2018). *Prefrontal cortex as a meta-reinforcement learning system.* Nature Neuroscience, 21, 860–868. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0147-8 — Shows how a recurrent network trained with reinforcement learning can learn to learn.
- Bandura, A., Ross, D. & Ross, S. A. (1961). *Transmission of aggression through imitation of aggressive models.* Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology, 63(3), 575–582. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045925 — The Bobo doll studies on observational learning.
- Pan, S. J. & Yang, Q. (2010). *A Survey on Transfer Learning.* IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering, 22(10). https://www.cse.ust.hk/~qyang/Docs/2009/tkde_transfer_learning.pdf — Defines transfer learning settings and reviews the main approaches to moving knowledge across tasks and domains.
- Kairouz, P., et al. (2021). *Advances and Open Problems in Federated Learning.* Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 14(1–2). https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977 — Surveys federated learning, its privacy and communication challenges, and open problems.
