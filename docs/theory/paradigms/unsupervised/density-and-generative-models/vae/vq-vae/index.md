# VQ-VAE

The vector-quantised variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) is a VAE whose latent codes are discrete. The encoder output is replaced by its nearest entry in a learned codebook, and a separate autoregressive prior models the codes, so that new images or audio can be generated by sampling codes and decoding them. It is one of the landmark variants described in [Variational Autoencoders](../index.md).

## Overview

The standard VAE uses continuous latents with Gaussian posteriors. Oord, Vinyals and Kavukcuoglu (2017) instead learn discrete codes, which keep high-level content and drop low-level detail. A powerful autoregressive model can then be fitted over the codes, which is cheaper than modelling every pixel. The authors also argue that discreteness helps avoid posterior collapse.

The posterior is deterministic: each encoder output maps to one code. The prior over codes is learned after the autoencoder has been trained, rather than fixed in advance.

## Key concepts

- **Codebook**: \(K\) learned vectors \(e_1,\dots,e_K\) in \(\mathbb{R}^D\), also called the embedding table. A discrete latent is an index into the codebook.
- **Nearest-neighbour quantisation**: the encoder output is replaced by the closest codebook vector, which makes the posterior one-hot.
- **Straight-through estimator**: the lookup has no useful gradient, so the gradient at the decoder input is copied to the encoder output.
- **Commitment**: a penalty that keeps encoder outputs close to the codebook vectors they select.
- **Autoregressive prior**: a PixelCNN for image codes or a WaveNet for audio, fitted to the codes after training.

## How it works

Quantisation selects the codebook vector nearest to the encoder output:

\[
k = \arg\min_j \lVert z_e(x) - e_j \rVert_2^2, \qquad z_q(x) = e_k
\]

The posterior is deterministic and the prior is uniform during training, so the KL term is a constant \(\log K\) and drops out of the objective. The training loss is

\[
L = -\log p\big(x \mid z_q(x)\big) + \lVert \mathrm{sg}[z_e(x)] - e_k \rVert_2^2 + \beta\,\lVert z_e(x) - \mathrm{sg}[e_k] \rVert_2^2
\]

The first term is the reconstruction loss, the second moves the selected codebook vector towards the encoder output, and the third is the commitment term. Here \(\mathrm{sg}\) is the stop-gradient operator, which is the identity in the forward pass and has zero derivative in the backward pass. The authors use \(\beta = 0.25\) and report that results did not vary for values from 0.1 to 2.0. Training follows these steps:

1. Encode the input to a grid of vectors \(z_e(x)\).
2. Replace each vector with its nearest codebook entry to form \(z_q(x)\).
3. Decode \(z_q(x)\) and compute the reconstruction loss. Pass its gradient to the encoder with the straight-through estimator.
4. Update the codebook with the second term, and the encoder with the third term.
5. Freeze the autoencoder, encode the data to code grids, and fit an autoregressive prior over the codes. To generate, sample a code grid from the prior and decode it.

## Results

- **Likelihood on CIFAR-10.** With the same architecture, the continuous VAE, the VQ-VAE and VIMCO obtain 4.51, 4.67 and 5.14 bits per dimension. All these values are lower bounds. The authors describe the VQ-VAE as the first discrete-latent model to challenge continuous VAEs on this comparison.
- **Images.** ImageNet images of 128 × 128 × 3 are compressed to a 32 × 32 × 1 grid of codes with \(K = 512\). The reconstructions look only slightly blurrier than the originals. A PixelCNN prior over the codes produces 128 × 128 samples.
- **Speech.** Mapping each code to its most likely phoneme gives 49.3% accuracy over 41 classes, against 7.2% for a baseline.

The paper reports likelihoods and samples. It does not report a sample-quality metric such as FID.

## Limitations

- **Two-stage training.** The prior is fitted after the autoencoder. The authors note that joint training could strengthen the results, and leave it for future work.
- **No true gradient.** The paper states that no real gradient is defined for the lookup, so the straight-through estimator is an approximation.
- **Blurry reconstructions.** A per-pixel loss makes reconstructions slightly blurry. The authors suggest a perceptual or adversarial loss as future work.

## Open questions

- Which part of the pipeline drives sample quality: the discrete bottleneck, the prior or the decoder? VQ-VAE-2 (Razavi et al., 2019) puts strong emphasis on the priors.

## Connections

- **Image tokenisers.** VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021) adds a patch-based discriminator and a perceptual loss to a convolutional VQ autoencoder, and models its codes with a Transformer.
- **Latent diffusion.** The autoencoder of [Latent Diffusion](../../diffusion/latent-diffusion.md) can be regularised with a KL penalty or with vector quantisation (Rombach et al., 2022).
- **Language tokenisation.** A VQ code plays a role close to a subword token, but it is learned from pixels or audio; compare [Tokenisation](../../../../../models/llm/tokenisation/index.md).

## Applications

- **Large-scale image generation.** VQ-VAE-2 (Razavi et al., 2019) builds a hierarchy of discrete codes. The authors report samples that rival GANs on several datasets.
- **Speech.** A WaveNet prior over the codes produced samples with recognisable words and phrases.

### Seminal papers

- Oord, A. van den, Vinyals, O. & Kavukcuoglu, K. (2017). *Neural Discrete Representation Learning*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00937 — The paper itself: the VQ-VAE, its training losses and its prior over codes.
- Kingma, D. P. & Welling, M. (2013). *Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114 — The continuous-latent VAE that the VQ-VAE replaces, and its baseline.
- Razavi, A., van den Oord, A. & Vinyals, O. (2019). *Generating Diverse High-Fidelity Images with VQ-VAE-2*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00446 — Scales the VQ-VAE with a hierarchy of discrete codes.
- Esser, P., Rombach, R. & Ommer, B. (2021). *Taming Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis*. CVPR. https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09841 — Learns a convolutional VQ codebook and models its codes with a Transformer.
- Rombach, R. et al. (2022). *High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models*. CVPR. https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752 — Compares KL-regularised and vector-quantised latent spaces for diffusion.
