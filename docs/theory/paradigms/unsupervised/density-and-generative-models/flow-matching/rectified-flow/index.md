# Rectified Flow

Rectified flow, introduced by Liu, Gong and Liu (2022), learns an ODE whose paths are as straight as possible between two empirical distributions. Repeating the procedure, called reflow, straightens the paths further. Straight paths can be followed with few Euler steps, which makes sampling fast. It is a member of the [Flow Matching and Rectified Flow](../index.md) family.

## Overview

Most generative flows curve from noise to data, so an ODE solver needs many small steps to follow them accurately. Liu et al. (2022) asked for the simplest possible flow instead. Given samples from two distributions, the method learns a velocity field whose trajectories follow the straight segments between paired points, as closely as a least-squares fit allows. Where the paths are straight, a single large Euler step is already a reasonable approximation.

The paper treats generation and transfer between two distributions, such as image-to-image translation, as one problem. Its distinctive contribution is reflow, which changes the coupling between the two distributions. The linear-path objective is a case of [conditional flow matching](../conditional-flow-matching/index.md).

## Key concepts

- **Linear interpolation**: the point \(X_t = tX_1 + (1-t)X_0\) between paired samples. Its velocity is \(X_1 - X_0\), which does not depend on \(t\).
- **Coupling**: the joint distribution of the pairs \((X_0, X_1)\). An arbitrary coupling is the usual starting point; a deterministic coupling comes from a learned flow.
- **Rectification**: learning a new flow from a coupling, in order to obtain a flow with straighter paths.
- **Reflow**: rectification repeated on pairs generated by the previous flow. Each round produces a new deterministic coupling.
- **Euler discretisation**: sampling by repeated steps \(Z_{k+1} = Z_k + \Delta t\, v(Z_k, t_k)\). Straight paths tolerate large \(\Delta t\).

## How it works

Let \(X_0\) and \(X_1\) be samples from the two distributions, paired by some coupling, and let \(X_t = tX_1 + (1-t)X_0\). Rectification finds the velocity field \(v\) that minimises the average squared error between the segment's velocity and the field:

\[
\min_v \int_0^1 \mathbb{E}\big[\,\|(X_1 - X_0) - v(X_t, t)\|^2\big]\,dt
\]

Because the target \(X_1 - X_0\) is fixed for each pair, this is an ordinary regression problem. Reflow then proceeds in rounds:

1. Train a rectified flow \(v\) on pairs from the current coupling, by the least-squares objective above.
2. Start the ODE \(dZ_t = v(Z_t, t)\,dt\) from many samples of the source distribution and integrate them, which yields new pairs \((Z_0, Z_1)\).
3. Use these pairs as the new coupling, and repeat from step 1.

The paper proves that rectification turns an arbitrary coupling into a deterministic one whose convex transport cost does not increase. Repeated reflow yields a sequence of flows with increasingly straight paths. Sampling then uses a coarse Euler discretisation, which the straight paths allow.

## Results

The abstract reports that rectified flow performs well on image generation, image-to-image translation and domain adaptation. For image generation and translation, the authors state that the learned flows are nearly straight and give high-quality results even with a single Euler step. The abstract does not name the datasets or report scores, so none are quoted here.

Later work tested these claims at larger scale. Esser et al. (2024) train rectified flow models with a Transformer for high-resolution text-to-image generation and report that the formulation outperforms established diffusion formulations. Liu et al. (2023) apply reflow to text-to-image generation with the aim of one-step sampling.

## Limitations

- **Reflow is costly.** Each round needs many ODE solves to generate the new pairs from the current model. For large models this is a substantial part of the training cost.
- **Straightness is approximate.** The learned velocity field follows straight segments only as well as the network and the training pairs allow.
- **Limited early evidence.** Liu et al. (2023) note that rectified flow had been applied only to small datasets. The large-scale evidence came later.
- **One-step quality.** A single step is a stronger requirement than a few steps. InstaFlow reports that distillation had not yet produced a functional one-step text-to-image model, and it explores rectified flow for that purpose.

## Open questions

- How many reflow rounds are worth their cost, and at what point does quality stop improving?
- Does straightness in the learned flow translate into better quality at the largest scales, or mainly into faster sampling?
- How should training times and noise levels be sampled? Esser et al. (2024) report that biasing this toward perceptually relevant scales helps.

## References

### Seminal papers

- Liu, X., Gong, C. & Liu, Q. (2022). *Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate and Transfer Data with Rectified Flow*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003 — The paper itself: the least-squares rectification objective, reflow and the image experiments described here.
- Lipman, Y., Chen, R. T. Q., Ben-Hamu, H., Nickel, M. & Le, M. (2022). *Flow Matching for Generative Modeling*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747 — The general flow-matching framework, of which rectified flow is the linear-path case.
- Liu, X. et al. (2023). *InstaFlow: One Step is Enough for High-Quality Diffusion-Based Text-to-Image Generation*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06380 — Applies rectified flow with reflow to text-to-image generation with the aim of one-step sampling.
- Esser, P. et al. (2024). *Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206 — Scales rectified flow with a Transformer for text-to-image generation, and studies the noise sampling used in training.

### Surveys and textbooks

- Holderrieth, P. & Erives, E. (2025). *An Introduction to Flow Matching and Diffusion Models*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02070 — A tutorial that covers the linear-path case of flow matching with the derivations behind it.
