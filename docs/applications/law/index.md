# Law

This page covers AI used **in** legal practice. For laws that regulate AI, see [Law, Policy & Governance](../../society/governance/index.md).

## Landscape

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Legal research | LLM-powered search over case law and statutes (Westlaw and Lexis AI tools, Harvey, CoCounsel) |
| Contract review and drafting | Clause extraction, risk flagging, redlining, playbook-based negotiation |
| E-discovery | Technology-assisted review (predictive coding) used in document review |
| Due diligence | Reviewing thousands of documents in M&A |
| Access to justice | Self-help tools, form filling, plain-language explanations |
| Courts | Case management, transcription, translation; some jurisdictions issued guidance on judges' use of AI |
| Legal prediction | Forecasting case outcomes (contested accuracy and ethics) |
| Compliance | Regulatory change tracking, policy mapping |

## The hallucination problem

- Lawyers have been sanctioned for filing briefs with fabricated case citations generated by AI tools
- Even specialised legal RAG tools hallucinated in a substantial share of queries in a Stanford evaluation (Magesh et al., 2024)
- Professional bodies have issued guidance on competence, confidentiality and verification when lawyers use generative AI

## Benchmarks

- GPT-4 passed the Uniform Bar Exam (Katz et al., 2023)
- LegalBench: a collaboratively built benchmark of legal reasoning tasks (Guha et al., 2023)
- Bar exams are a weak proxy for legal practice

## Risks in high-stakes legal decisions

- **Risk assessment in criminal justice**: the COMPAS tool was found to have racial disparities in error rates (Chouldechova, 2017), which illustrates trade-offs between fairness definitions (see [Ethics & Fairness](../../society/ethics/index.md))
- **Due process**: right to explanation and contestability
- **Confidentiality and privilege** when uploading client documents
- **Unauthorised practice of law** questions for consumer legal AI

## Key methods

Three methods are most relevant here. Each links to the page that explains it.

- [Transformer](../../theory/models/transformer/attention-is-all-you-need/index.md): the architecture behind the LLMs that power legal research and drafting tools (Vaswani et al., 2017).
- [InstructGPT and RLHF](../../theory/models/llm/instructgpt-rlhf/index.md): instruction tuning on human feedback, the basis of most legal assistant products (Ouyang et al., 2022).
- [Retrieval-augmented generation](../../engineering/rag/index.md): grounding answers in retrieved case law and statutes, which reduces but does not eliminate hallucination (Magesh et al., 2024).

## References

### Seminal papers

- Magesh et al. (2024). Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362 — Evaluates leading legal research tools and finds hallucinations in many queries.
- Katz et al. (2023). GPT-4 Passes the Bar Exam. *SSRN*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4389233 — Reports GPT-4 performance on the Uniform Bar Exam.
- Guha et al. (2023). LegalBench: A Collaboratively Built Benchmark for Measuring Legal Reasoning in Large Language Models. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11462 — A collaboratively built benchmark of legal reasoning tasks.
- Chouldechova (2017). Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact: A Study of Bias in Recidivism Prediction Instruments. *Big Data*. https://doi.org/10.1089/big.2016.0047 — Studies recidivism risk tools and the trade-offs between fairness definitions.
- Vaswani, A. et al. (2017). *Attention Is All You Need*. NeurIPS. https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762 — Introduces the Transformer, built on multi-head self-attention.
- Ouyang, L. et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. NeurIPS. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155 — InstructGPT, instruction tuning from human preference feedback.

### Surveys and textbooks

- Surden (2025). Artificial Intelligence and Law – An Overview of Recent Technological Changes in Large Language Models and Law. *SSRN*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5135305 — Overview of how large language models are changing legal practice.
