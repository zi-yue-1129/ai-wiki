# Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

Model-based reinforcement learning learns a model of the environment's dynamics and uses it to plan or to generate imagined experience. It can be far more sample-efficient than model-free methods, at the cost of model errors that the agent must handle.

## Overview

A learned model predicts the next state and reward from the current state and action. The agent can then plan by simulating candidate action sequences, or train its policy on imagined rollouts. The risk is that errors compound over long rollouts, so the model is only as useful as the states it has been trained on.

## Main approaches

- **Dyna** (Sutton, 1991) interleaves real experience with simulated experience generated by a learned model.
- **Model-predictive control** plans with the model over a short horizon and re-plans at every step, which is common in robotics.
- **World models** (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) learn a compact latent representation of the environment and train a small controller entirely inside the model.
- **MuZero** learns only the quantities needed for planning, namely rewards, values and policies, rather than full observations, and it reaches strong performance in board games and Atari.
- **Dreamer** learns a latent dynamics model and trains an actor and critic on imagined trajectories through it.

## Model errors

Compounding error in long rollouts is the central problem. Methods reduce it with short horizons, ensembles of models that estimate uncertainty, and training the model on states the policy actually visits.

## Connections

Planning with a model relates to [value-based methods](../value-based-methods/index.md), since learned values often guide the search. Learned world models are a form of [self-supervised](../../self-supervised/index.md) prediction of the future, and the same idea appears in [world models and embodied AI](../../../../models/world-models/index.md). Neuroscience links planning to hippocampal replay, see [computational neuroscience](../../../../brain-mind/computational-neuroscience/index.md).

## Applications

Robot manipulation with few real trials, continuous control with learned dynamics, game-playing agents that plan with learned rules, and industrial process control where experiments are expensive.

## Open questions

Models that generalise beyond their training distribution, and methods that give calibrated uncertainty about the model, are still needed. Choosing when to trust imagined rollouts over real experience is an open practical problem.
