# Law, Policy & Governance

!!! note "Status as of October 2026"
    AI law is changing quickly. Check official sources for current obligations; this page is an orientation, not legal advice.

## European Union: the AI Act

The first comprehensive horizontal AI law (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), in force since August 2024 with phased application.

| Risk tier | Examples | Obligations |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable (prohibited, from Feb 2025) | Social scoring by public authorities, manipulative techniques causing harm, untargeted scraping for facial-recognition databases, emotion recognition in workplaces and schools, most real-time remote biometric identification by police in public | Banned |
| High-risk | AI in medical devices, critical infrastructure, education, employment, credit, law enforcement, migration, justice | Risk management, data governance, documentation, human oversight, accuracy and robustness, conformity assessment, registration |
| Limited risk (transparency) | Chatbots, deepfakes, AI-generated content | Disclosure and labelling |
| Minimal risk | Spam filters, games | None beyond existing law |
| General-purpose AI models (from Aug 2025) | Foundation models | Technical documentation, copyright policy, training-data summary; extra duties (evaluations, incident reporting, cybersecurity) for models with systemic risk (presumed above 10²⁵ training FLOPs); a voluntary Code of Practice published in 2025 |

Fines reach up to 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices. In late 2025 the Commission proposed a "digital omnibus" that would delay some high-risk obligations; check the current timetable.

## United States

- No comprehensive federal AI law; governance through executive actions, agency guidance and sector regulators (FDA, FTC, EEOC, CFPB)
- Executive Order 14110 (2023) was revoked in January 2025; the 2025 *AI Action Plan* emphasised innovation, infrastructure and exports
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) and its generative-AI profile are widely used voluntary standards; the US AI Safety Institute was reorganised as the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) in 2025
- **States**: Colorado AI Act (algorithmic discrimination in consequential decisions; effective date delayed to 2026), California's SB 53 (2025) on frontier-model transparency and safety-incident reporting, plus many deepfake, election and chatbot laws
- Export controls on advanced chips and model weights; see [Compute Governance and Export Controls](compute-governance.md)

## China

- Sector-specific, fast-moving regulation: algorithmic recommendation rules (2022), deep synthesis rules (2023), Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (2023) requiring security assessments and filing, labelling rules for AI-generated content (effective September 2025)
- Algorithm registry with the Cyberspace Administration of China

## Other jurisdictions

| Jurisdiction | Approach |
|---|---|
| United Kingdom | Principles-based, regulator-led; AI Security Institute for frontier-model evaluation |
| Canada | Proposed AIDA lapsed in 2025; voluntary code for generative AI |
| Japan | AI Promotion Act (2025), light-touch, plus guidelines |
| South Korea | AI Basic Act (passed late 2024, effective January 2026) |
| Taiwan | AI Basic Act drafted by the National Science and Technology Council, under legislative review; sector guidance for government and finance |
| Brazil | Comprehensive AI bill (PL 2338/2023) advancing |

## International efforts

- **OECD AI Principles** (2019, updated 2024) and G7 **Hiroshima Process** code of conduct
- **UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI** (2021)
- **Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI** (2024) — the first binding international AI treaty
- AI safety summits: Bletchley Park (2023), Seoul (2024), Paris AI Action Summit (2025)
- **UN**: General Assembly resolutions; an Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and a Global Dialogue on AI Governance established in 2025
- Standards: ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems), ISO/IEC 23894 (risk), IEEE 7000 series, CEN-CENELEC harmonised standards for the AI Act

## Key policy debates

- Open-weight vs closed models

- Liability for AI harms
