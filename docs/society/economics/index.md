# Economics & Work

## AI as a general-purpose technology

Like electricity or computing, AI may affect most sectors, with productivity gains that arrive slowly as organisations reinvent workflows (Brynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson's "productivity J-curve").

## Evidence on productivity

| Study | Setting | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2023/2025, *QJE*) | Customer-support agents with an AI assistant | ~14–15% more issues resolved per hour on average; ~30%+ for novices, little for top performers |
| Noy & Zhang (2023, *Science*) | Professional writing tasks with ChatGPT | ~40% less time, higher quality |
| Dell'Acqua et al. (2023) | BCG consultants with GPT-4 | Faster and better on tasks within the AI's frontier; *worse* on a task outside it ("jagged frontier") |
| Peng et al. (2023) | Developers with Copilot | ~56% faster on a coding task |
| METR (2025) | Experienced open-source developers on their own repos | ~19% slower with early-2025 tools |

Pattern: large gains on well-specified tasks and for less experienced workers; smaller or negative effects on complex expert work; aggregate effects so far modest but growing.

## Labour market exposure

- Eloundou et al. (2023, "GPTs are GPTs"): around 80% of US workers have at least 10% of tasks exposed to LLMs; higher-wage, cognitive occupations are more exposed — unlike earlier automation waves
- IMF (2024): about 40% of global employment is exposed, ~60% in advanced economies, with both complementarity and substitution
- Exposure ≠ job loss: tasks change, new tasks appear (Autor's work on "new work")
- Early evidence: declines in some freelance writing and translation demand; some studies find falling entry-level hiring in highly exposed occupations since 2022, though causes are debated
- Usage studies of AI assistants (e.g. Anthropic Economic Index, 2025) show software development and writing dominate current use, with a mix of automation and augmentation

## Macro questions

- **Growth**: estimates of AI's contribution to annual productivity growth range from modest (Acemoglu, 2024: ~0.7% total TFP over a decade) to transformative
- **Inequality**: gains may flow to capital owners and highly skilled workers, or compress skill gaps within occupations
- **Market concentration**: compute, data and talent advantages favour large firms; open-weight models and falling inference prices push the other way
- **Prices of intelligence**: the cost per token for a given capability level has fallen by orders of magnitude per year
- **Transformative-AI scenarios**: if AI automates AI research, growth could accelerate dramatically — highly uncertain and debated

## Policy responses

Education and reskilling, wage insurance, portable benefits, competition policy, tax policy (capital vs labour), public compute and data infrastructure, and ideas such as universal basic income.

## References

- Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D. & Raymond, L. (2025). Generative AI at Work. *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044 — Field study of customer-support agents in which access to a generative AI assistant raised productivity, with the largest gains for less experienced workers.
- Noy, S. & Zhang, W. (2023). Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence. *Science*. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586 — Randomised experiment on professional writing tasks finding large time savings and quality gains when participants used ChatGPT.
- Eloundou, T. et al. (2023). *GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models*. arXiv:2303.10130. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130 — Rates how far large language models could affect each task in occupations, giving the exposure measures used in this section.
- Acemoglu, D. & Restrepo, P. (2019). Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor. *Journal of Economic Perspectives*. https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.3 — Separates the displacement effect of automation from the creation of new tasks that reinstate labour.
- Felten, E., Raj, M. & Seamans, R. (2021). Occupational, industry, and geographic exposure to artificial intelligence: A novel dataset and its potential uses. *Strategic Management Journal*. https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.3286 — Builds occupation-level AI exposure scores from ability linkages, a dataset that many later labour-market studies use.
