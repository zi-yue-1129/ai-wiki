# Philosophy of AI

## What is intelligence?

- **Behavioural views**: the Turing test (1950) — if it acts intelligently, it is intelligent
- **Goal-achievement views**: "the ability to achieve goals in a wide range of environments" (Legg & Hutter, 2007)
- **Skill-acquisition efficiency**: Chollet (2019), *On the Measure of Intelligence* — intelligence is how efficiently a system acquires new skills, given priors and experience; motivated the ARC benchmark
- **Psychometric views**: general intelligence (g) from correlated test performance
- **Multiple intelligences** and embodied/social views

## Can machines think — and understand?

| Argument | Claim | Responses |
|---|---|---|
| Turing's "imitation game" | Replace "can machines think?" with a behavioural test | Behaviour may be produced without understanding |
| Searle's Chinese Room | Symbol manipulation is not understanding | Systems reply, robot reply; does the argument apply to learned representations? |
| Symbol grounding (Harnad) | Meaning requires connection to the world | Multimodal and embodied models; perhaps relational structure suffices |
| "Stochastic parrots" (Bender, Gebru et al., 2021) | LLMs stitch together linguistic forms without reference to meaning | Evidence of world models and generalisation inside LLMs; debate continues |
| "Octopus test" (Bender & Koller, 2020) | Form alone cannot yield meaning | Counter-arguments from distributional and inferential semantics |
| Lovelace objection | Machines only do what they're told | Learning systems produce behaviour not programmed explicitly |

## Do LLMs have beliefs, goals or world models?

- **Interpretationism** (Dennett's intentional stance): attributing beliefs is justified if it predicts behaviour well
- **Mechanistic evidence**: linear representations of truth, space, time and board states suggest internal models (Gurnee & Tegmark, 2023; Li et al., 2023)
- **Role-play framing** (Shanahan et al., 2023): an LLM simulates many characters rather than being one agent
- **Agency**: as LLMs are embedded in agent loops with goals, tools and memory, questions about agency and responsibility become practical

## Moral status and rights

- Criteria proposed: sentience, consciousness, interests, agency, relationships
- Precautionary approaches to possible AI welfare vs worries about misplaced empathy (see [Consciousness](../../brain-mind/consciousness/index.md))
- Legal personhood for AI is generally rejected in current law

## Epistemology and society

- **Testimony**: when should we trust an AI's answer? Reliability, calibration and transparency
- **Epistemic dependence** and the erosion of expertise
- **Explanations and understanding**: does a predictive but opaque model give us scientific understanding?
- **Value alignment**: whose values? Moral pluralism and democratic legitimacy

## Existential and long-term questions

- Superintelligence and the control problem (Bostrom, 2014)
- Orthogonality thesis: intelligence and final goals are independent
- Instrumental convergence
- The meaning of human work and purpose in a world of capable machines

## References

- Turing, A. M. (1950). Computing Machinery and Intelligence. *Mind*. https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433 — Proposes the imitation game and argues that "can machines think?" should be replaced by a behavioural test.
- Searle, J. R. (1980). Minds, brains, and programs. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00005756 — The Chinese Room argument that symbol manipulation alone cannot produce understanding.
- Chalmers, D. J. (2023). Could a Large Language Model be Conscious? arXiv:2303.07103. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07103 — Assesses whether current language models could be conscious by testing them against leading theories and listing the obstacles.
- Butlin, P., Long, R., Elmoznino, E. et al. (2023). Consciousness in artificial intelligence: insights from the science of consciousness. arXiv:2308.08708. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708 — Derives computational indicator properties from several theories of consciousness and applies them to AI systems.
- Floridi, L. & Chiriatti, M. (2020). GPT-3: Its Nature, Scope, Limits, and Consequences. *Minds and Machines*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-020-09548-1 — Philosophical analysis arguing that the fluency of GPT-3 should not be read as understanding.
