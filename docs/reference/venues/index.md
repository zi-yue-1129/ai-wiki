# Conferences and Journals

Where AI research is published, how the main venues differ, and how to read a venue's name. The venues below are the ones most often cited in this wiki. Each link goes to the venue's own site or to its proceedings archive.

| [ICML](https://icml.cc/) | International Conference on Machine Learning | Conference site |
| [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/) | Learning representations, including deep learning and its applications | Conference site |

## Preprints and open platforms

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/about) was founded in 1991 by Paul Ginsparg. It is an open platform for sharing research, and it now hosts nearly two million papers across physics, mathematics, computer science and other fields. Many AI papers appear on arXiv first or alongside a conference submission, so a paper's arXiv version is often the easiest copy to find.

[OpenReview](https://openreview.net/) is a platform for transparent sharing and review of research. It hosts academic venues, including conferences and journals, and it makes reviews visible for some of them.

## Review processes

Peer review can be single-blind, where reviewers see the authors' names, or double-blind, where they do not. The two models can produce different reviewer behaviour. [Tomkins et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1707323114) study reviewer bias under single-blind and double-blind review, which bears on how far a venue's decisions can be trusted as a measure of quality.

## How to read a venue's name

- A venue name signals a subject area and a community. It is not a quality grade on its own.
- Venues differ in scope, in review policy and in how much of their archive is open. Check the venue's own site before relying on its process.
- Some strong work appears first on arXiv, in a workshop or in a specialist venue, so a paper's absence from a top conference is not evidence that it is weak.
